HyTech Racing: HT11 FSAE Electric Racecar

Georgia TechElectrical Subsystem Team MemberAug 2026 – Present

HyTech Racing designs and builds a Formula SAE Electric racecar every year to compete against university teams from around the world. I'm on the electrical subteam, which develops the car's low voltage and high voltage systems and the firmware that runs them.

What I work on

  • Charging Control Unit unit tests: writing unit test code that creates a virtual test environment, so firmware can be validated before it's ever flashed to hardware.
  • Dashboard Ethernet: designing and testing an Ethernet implementation on the rev 9 vehicle dashboard to speed up data transfer and support more demanding display processing.
  • Onboarding projects: PCB design in Altium and C++ firmware in PlatformIO to collect temperature data.

Why it matters

On a racecar, a firmware bug found on track costs testing time, and on a high voltage system it can be a safety risk. Testing code virtually before it reaches the car helps the team find problems early and iterate faster.

Innovation Academy Motorsports: Two Electric Racecars

Founder & Team LeadSpecialized Electrical SystemsJan 2025 – May 2026

My high school had never had a motorsports team, so I founded one. We designed and built two Electrathon-style electric racecars with custom aluminum chassis and entered high school racing divisions for the first time.

The problem

We were starting with no team, no car and no previous designs, and the cars had to be safe, efficient and competitive on a limited student budget.

My role and process

  • Led the electrical systems: designed the battery system, driver controls and all circuit wiring for both cars.
  • Used CAD and CFD analysis to shape the chassis and body for better aerodynamics.
  • Worked on chassis manufacturing and welding, the drivetrain and body paneling.

The outcome

Both cars were completed and raced. The team placed 9th at the state-level competition and 1st in our local intraschool competition in its first season. Building these cars is what pointed me toward HyTech Racing.

First Person View (FPV) Drones

Personal projectJan – Apr 2025

I engineered and programmed two FPV drones from scratch. These are fast, manually flown quadcopters piloted through a live video feed from an onboard camera.

The process

  • Selected and assembled the frame, motors, electronic speed controllers, flight controller, radio receiver and video system.
  • Soldered and integrated the electronics for each build.
  • Configured and tuned the flight controller in Betaflight, setting up rates, modes and control response for stable, responsive flight.

The outcome

Two flying drones, and a much better feel for how sensors, control loops and power systems work together in an aircraft. The same skills helped me win 1st place in the TSA State Drone UAV Challenge and earn my FAA Part 107 remote pilot license.

Multipurpose Video Gimbal

Personal projectJan – Apr 2025

Alongside the drones, I designed a 3D-printed, multipurpose gimbal that keeps a camera steady for smooth video footage.

The process

  • Designed the gimbal structure in CAD and iterated on it through several prototypes.
  • Printed the parts on Bambu Lab printers.
  • Programmed and tuned the gimbal's motor controller with SimpleBGC so it would hold the camera level.

The outcome

A working stabilized gimbal, built from my own design, that combines mechanical design, motor control and electronics in one system.

Arduino Video Game Controller

Personal projectJan – Mar 2025

I built a fully custom video game controller, from the electronics to the case, powered by an Arduino Nano.

The process

  • Programmed the Arduino Nano to read the inputs and carry out game operations.
  • Integrated multiple joysticks and buttons and soldered every component by hand.
  • Prototyped the casing through several rounds of CAD design and modification, then 3D printed it on a Bambu Lab X1 Carbon.

The outcome

A working, comfortable controller and my first complete product, from circuit to firmware to enclosure.

CO2 Dragster Racecar

TSA competitionNov 2024 – Mar 2026

A lightweight, aerodynamic shell dragster powered by a CO2 cartridge and designed for maximum straight-line speed.

The process

  • Designed the body in CAD with a focus on low drag and low weight.

The outcome

The final design placed 8th at the TSA State competition, and it taught me how to iterate using both simulation and physical testing.
