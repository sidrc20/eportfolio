Career goals

Where I'm headed, and how I'll get there.

My long-term goal, and the plan for getting from a sophomore on a racing team to leading electronics R&D.

Long-term goal

I want to work on the technical development of electronics in automotive, aviation or chip design, doing research and design work and eventually leading innovation in these fields.

Automotive electronics

Electric vehicles depend on battery management, power electronics and embedded control. My work on two Electrathon cars and HyTech's HT11 is my starting point.

Aerospace & UAV systems

Avionics, flight controllers and autonomous drones. My FAA Part 107 license and FPV builds gave me hands-on experience with flight electronics.

Semiconductors & chip design

Every vehicle and aircraft runs on custom silicon. I want to go from gate-level logic and CMOS fundamentals to designing real integrated circuits.

Roadmap

From here to there

Each stage builds technical depth, industry experience and leadership, the three things I'll need to lead R&D teams.

NOWSophomore year

Build the foundation

Get strong in the core EE curriculum while getting real engineering experience on a competition team.

  • Keep a strong GPA through the core circuits, digital systems and signals courses.
  • Grow at HyTech Racing, moving from onboarding projects to owning a subsystem, such as the Charging Control Unit test framework and dashboard Ethernet.
  • Build PCB design skills in Altium and embedded C++ firmware skills on real vehicle hardware.
  • Apply for summer EE internships and connect with recruiters at the ECE Career Fair.
NEXTJunior year

Go deeper and get industry experience

Specialize, and test which of my three fields fits me best.

  • Complete an internship or co-op in automotive, aerospace or semiconductor electronics.
  • Join an undergraduate research lab in power electronics, embedded systems or VLSI.
  • Take electives in microelectronics, digital design and power systems.
  • Take on a leadership role within HyTech's electrical team.
THENSenior year

Lead and specialize

Turn experience into ownership and choose a direction for after graduation.

  • Lead a senior design project focused on vehicle or flight electronics, or on chip design.
  • Complete a second internship, aiming for a full-time return offer.
  • Decide between going straight into industry or pursuing a graduate degree (M.S.) to specialize in research and design.
AFTER 2029Career

Design, then lead

Begin as a design engineer, then grow into leading R&D.

  • Start as a hardware or electrical design engineer on an EV, aerospace or semiconductor team.
  • Build a record of shipped designs and contributions to new technology.
  • Move into technical leadership and lead the R&D teams that shape the next generation of vehicle, aircraft and chip electronics.
