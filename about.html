Hey there!

I'm Siddharth Chintalapalli, though most people call me Sid. I'm an Electrical Engineering student at the Georgia Institute of Technology, and I'm happiest when an idea leaves the whiteboard and turns into hardware that actually moves, flies or computes.

How it started

My passion for electrical engineering began in a high school class on Arduino. The projects we built there were the first time I saw code I'd written make something happen in the physical world, like spinning a motor, reading a sensor or lighting up a display. I didn't want to stop when the class ended. I took what I'd learned home and started building my own passion projects: a custom video game controller, FPV drones I programmed from scratch, and a motorized camera gimbal.

The more I built, the more I wanted to understand why things work. Watching theory turn into a working circuit is still what fascinates me most about this field. It's the reason I now study digital logic, CMOS networks and circuit design alongside the hands-on work.

Why racecars

In high school, our school didn't have a motorsports program, so I founded one. Innovation Academy Motorsports built two custom aluminum-chassis electric racecars and competed in high school racing divisions for the first time in the school's history. I led the electrical systems, including the battery, driver controls and wiring, and I also learned to weld, design for aerodynamics and run CFD. We placed 9th at the state level and 1st in our local competition.

That experience is what brought me to HyTech Racing, Georgia Tech's Formula SAE Electric team. I'm on the electrical subteam, where I work on firmware, PCB design and testing for the HT11 racecar.

What I'm looking for

I'm currently seeking internships in electrical engineering. I want professional experience in industry, to learn from experts, and to build the technical skills I need to contribute to the field. I'm also exploring undergraduate research, because I want to be part of the teams pushing electrical engineering into new applications, especially in automotive, aviation and semiconductor design.

Beyond engineering

I've played soccer my whole life. It was my biggest passion growing up and it taught me most of what I know about teamwork. These days I play a variety of sports, go hiking, work out, and I'm teaching myself to DJ. On campus, I'm a member of the Delta Chi fraternity in addition to HyTech Racing.
